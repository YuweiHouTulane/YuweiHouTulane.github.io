About

I'm a PhD student in biomedical informatics at the Tulane Center for Biomedical Informatics and Genomics, in the John W. Deming Department of Medicine, advised by Hui Shen in Hong-Wen Deng's group. I work on the genetic and cellular basis of skeletal disease — osteoporosis above all, alongside an interest in Alzheimer's disease — combining GWAS with single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics. Some of that is bench work and some of it is analysis; I'd rather understand both ends of the data I work with.

I came to this from clinical medicine. I trained as a physician at Dalian Medical University and spent three years as a resident in surgical departments, then took an MPH in biostatistics and epidemiology at Drexel. Before returning to research I spent four years managing data for clinical trials, latterly at Sanofi — years of cleaning trial data that still shape how I read a dataset.

I'm always glad to hear from people working on related questions.

Research interests

  • Statistical genetics Genome-wide association analysis of skeletal traits, and the work of moving from an associated variant toward a mechanism.
  • Single-cell & spatial transcriptomics Cell-type heterogeneity in bone and marrow, and the ligand–receptor networks connecting neighboring populations. 10x Visium and BGI Stereo-seq.
  • Osteoporosis & skeletal aging Why bone is lost with age, and how bone, marrow, and skeletal muscle behave as one communicating system rather than three separate tissues.
  • Population health Multifactorial modeling of large survey data to work out what actually drives geographic disparities in chronic disease risk.
