About

I'm a PhD student at the Tulane Center for Biomedical Informatics and Genomics, in the John W. Deming Department of Medicine. My work applies spatial and single-cell transcriptomics to the musculoskeletal system — including a recent spatially resolved map of the signaling that connects bone to the skeletal muscle beside it.

A second thread of my work is population-scale: using multifactorial modeling and machine learning on national survey data to ask why the burden of chronic disease varies so sharply from one part of the United States to another.

Research interests

  • Spatial & single-cell transcriptomics Deconvolving cell-type composition in tissue sections, and inferring the ligand–receptor networks that connect neighboring cell populations.
  • Musculoskeletal biology Bone, marrow, and skeletal muscle as one communicating system — and how that crosstalk breaks down in conditions like osteoporosis and sarcopenia.
  • Population health & machine learning Multifactorial modeling of large survey data to work out what actually drives geographic disparities in chronic disease risk.
